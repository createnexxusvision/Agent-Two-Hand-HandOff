# Ship PayPal's Missing Agent Payouts Layer, Provably

Agent Two Hand HandOff should keep its architecture and rewrite its claims. The pitch can rest on one checkable, high-confidence fact: **PayPal's Agent Toolkit and MCP server have no Payouts tool, and every PayPal agentic product found is buy-side**, so a PayPal-native way for agents to pay people is still open ([paypal/agent-toolkit](https://github.com/paypal/agent-toolkit), High; [PayPal agent tools reference](https://developer.paypal.com/ai-tools/agent-tools), High). The October 8 strategy's broader whitespace claim, that nobody offers compliant fiat agent-to-human payouts, does not survive. Payouts.com markets agent payouts with KYC, 1099 and sanctions checks in preview, Upwork's MCP server lets agents hire identity-verified freelancers, and Grade (YC W26) runs creator payroll. PayPal's own hackathon resources even suggest paying contractors automatically when a job is done, in front of **13,720 registrants**. What nobody ships, and no standard covers, is a payout bound to a creator-signed labor mandate, gated by an independent verifier, whose receipts anyone can verify offline: AP2 lists push payments as roadmap, and every evidence format is a draft that ignores labor. On stage, drop Cloudflare's 57.5% figure and Visa's 450% figure (which is 2025 data, not H1 2026), and lead instead with four 2026 primary statistics on who holds payment authority and how fast agent traffic is growing. The rules weigh five criteria equally, with Technological Implementation as the first tie-break. They require a public YouTube video and a hosted demo that judges can use without their own AI key through **Dec 15**, and they allow one Grand Prize or one Honorable Mention plus one sponsor prize. **Midnight is not a sponsor**, and AG Grid's $5,000 prize asks for AG Studio dashboards, not a plain grid. The highest-return changes are to fix how Payouts failures are simulated, make the video public, move Midnight's 8 hours into Merkle checkpoints with ML-DSA co-signatures and external anchoring (11–18 hours by the notes' estimates), reshape the mandate after AP2, reframe the 1099 tracker around the 1099-K rule, and build on AG Studio.

### The stage set measures payment authority, not market size

The strongest stage statistics are primary-sourced, from 2026, and about **who holds the authority to pay**. That is exactly the problem the mandate, policy engine and approval threshold solve.

| # | Statistic | Suggested wording | Source, method, confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **23%** of U.S. consumers trust GenAI to handle payments on their behalf | "In a Harris Poll for Visa this May, only 23% of U.S. consumers said they trust AI to handle payments for them." | [Visa Trust Index, Sep 9 2026](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22736.html); n=2,065, fielded May 26–28, 2026; High |
| 2 | **60%** would not let AI spend any amount without approval; only 28% trust independent AI agents | "60% of U.S. adults say they would not let AI spend any amount without their approval. | 4 | More than half of traffic to Cloudflare sites is automated; **daily AI-agent requests up 1,700%+** in a year | "Cloudflare reported in September that more than half the traffic reaching sites on its network is now automated, and daily AI-agent requests grew more than 1,700% in a year." | [Cloudflare, Sep 30 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agentic-web/); High |

Three cautions apply. First, Visa also reports that 61% would trust Visa with agentic transactions; that figure is brand-specific, so at a PayPal event it should only illustrate that trust rises when an established network secures the transaction, never imply anything about PayPal (inference). Second, PayPal's own merchant survey finds that nearly two-thirds of merchants want a standardized liability framework like card-not-present chargeback rules ([PayPal Agentic Commerce Pulse, Apr 2026](https://www.paypal.com/us/brc/article/agentic-commerce-pulse-report-findings), High), a natural fit for the evidence pitch in front of PayPal judges, but its segment counts sum to 598 against a stated 498 respondents, so cite the headline and not the segments. Third, **no primary statistic measures agents paying humans for work**: the category appears unmeasured, so the pitch should not size an agent-payroll market and should take "who it's for" from creator-economy data instead.

The pattern behind these numbers is itself a usable narrative. Agent traffic is exploding: Cloudflare's 1,700%, and Adobe's +393% year-over-year AI-referred retail traffic in Q1 2026 ([Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-surge-retail-sites-not-machine-readable), Medium). Trust in agent payments is flat or falling: Riskified's series moved from roughly 70% at least somewhat comfortable in Q4 2025 to 55% not comfortable in Q1 2026, though the samples differ ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/many-consumers-say-no-thanks-to-agent-based-payments/), Medium). The bottleneck is **authority, not capability** (inference).

## The rules reward a working PayPal core, a public video and one sponsor prize

### Technological Implementation decides every tie

Submissions close **Thursday Nov 12, 2026 at 12:00 pm PT**; judging runs Dec 1–15, and winners are announced around Dec 21 ([Official Rules](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules), High). HANDOFF's dates are right. Stage One is a pass/fail check that the project reasonably fits the theme and reasonably applies the featured PayPal APIs. Stage Two scores Technological Implementation, Design, Potential Impact, Innovation/Idea and Presentation with **equal weight, breaking ties in that order**, and the rules allow expert panels, peer review or **automated AI-driven analysis** to do the judging (High). Two consequences follow (inference). The README must be readable by a machine reviewer, with an explicit table of PayPal APIs used and an explicit AI-tools section. And PayPal depth (a real batch, a verified webhook, idempotency and failure handling) is worth more than breadth, because it settles ties. The Innovation criterion asks whether the concept differs from existing ones, which matters because PayPal itself seeded this concept (next section).

The submission requirements are mostly in HANDOFF already, with one correction. The project must integrate the PayPal sandbox with an AI tool, with PayPal central ([Rules §4](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules), High). Judges must be able to run a working build, from repo instructions or a hosted URL, and mockups or static prototypes fail (High). The repo must be public, with an open-source license visible in the About panel (High). The video must run under three minutes (judges need not watch further), contain no third-party trademarks or copyrighted music without permission, and be **publicly visible on YouTube** (High); HANDOFF §7.3's "public or unlisted" is the correction. The submission must also name which AI tools were used and how ([Overview](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/), High). The Devpost manager added that judges won't have their own Anthropic key, that a hosted demo is the easiest path, that live keys must stay out of the public repo, and that the project must stay testable, free, until **Dec 15** ([Discussion: API Key for Judges](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/forum_topics/45465-api-key-for-judges), High). That makes the Render deployment with server-held keys effectively mandatory, and it stretches the hosting budget a month past the deadline. Existing projects must show significant updates after Oct 1 at 9:15 am PT, and anyone employed by a judge's company is excluded ([Rules §3–4](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/rules), High); if any repo content predates Oct 1, the submission should say what changed (inference).

The demo should show a handled failure, not only the happy path (inference).

### PayPal's agent tooling stops at checkout, and Payouts has its own test harness

Every PayPal agentic product found serves merchants selling to AI shoppers. These include Agentic Commerce Services with Agent Ready and Store Sync, launched Oct 28, 2025 ([PayPal Newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2025-10-28-PayPal-Launches-Agentic-Commerce-Services-to-Power-AI-Driven-Shopping), Medium), and ACP with OpenAI and UCP with Google, both through Agent Ready for **Braintree merchants** ([Agent Ready docs](https://developer.paypal.com/agent-ready/overview), High). They also include Mastercard Agent Pay, Perplexity Instant Buy and Microsoft Copilot Checkout. The Agent Toolkit's tool groups are Invoices (20 tools), Payments, Disputes, Shipment, Catalog, Subscriptions and Reporting, with **no Payouts tool** ([GitHub](https://github.com/paypal/agent-toolkit), High). The new AI-Toolkit is a coding-agent plugin with the same categories ([GitHub paypal/AI-Toolkit](https://github.com/paypal/AI-Toolkit), Medium). PayPal's newsroom from April 2025 to October 2026 has no release about agents paying people ([newsroom listing](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/news?l=100), Medium).

Two PayPal precedents are worth using. First, PayPal's May 2026 Claude for Small Business plugin requires owner approval before anything is sent or paid ([PayPal Newsroom](https://newsroom.paypal-corp.com/2026-05-PayPal-partners-with-Anthropic-to-Close-the-AI-Gap-for-Small-Businesses), Medium), which makes the plan's approval threshold consistent with PayPal's stated stance. Second, a live sandbox bug: a participant reports that the sandbox MCP server's `create_invoice` has failed since about Oct 6, with no fix posted ([Discussion](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/forum_topics/45463-paypal-sandbox-mcp-error), High), so direct REST should stay the default for Invoicing. Note also that the hackathon Resources page's docs.paypal.ai links redirect to 404s; the live pages are under developer.paypal.com/ai-tools (High).

Sandbox Payouts works **without production approval** ([Payouts setup](https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/use-payouts/set-up), High), but its testing model differs from what HANDOFF assumes. `PayPal-Mock-Response` is documented only for the Payments and Orders APIs ([negative-testing headers](https://developer.paypal.com/negative-testing/request-headers), High). Payouts instead uses case-sensitive test values ([Payouts test and go live](https://developer.paypal.com/api/payouts/test-go-live/), High): in `items[0].note` on create (for example `ERRPYO005` for INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS), in the URI path on item lookup (`ERRPYO047` for RECEIVER_UNREGISTERED), and in the URI path on batch lookup (`ERRPYOB004` for DENIED).

The other facts that matter for the build are these. A `sender_batch_id` reused within 30 days is rejected, which makes it a natural idempotency key ([schema](https://developer.paypal.com/api/payments.payouts-batch/v1/schema.yaml), Medium). The sandbox does not support the PHONE recipient type, and Venmo payouts are US/USD only and need a note (Medium). Unclaimed payouts return to the sender after **30 days** ([payment processing](https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/payment-processing), High). The default per-item limit is $20,000, and the sender pays a capped percentage fee ([Payouts FAQs](https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/faqs), High). Rate limits are unpublished, so callers should honor `Retry-After` and reuse OAuth tokens ([rate limiting](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rate-limiting), High).

Webhooks need the most care. Simulator (mock) events **cannot** be verified through the verify-webhook-signature postback, and PayPal retries undelivered events up to 25 times over 3 days ([Webhooks](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/rest/), Medium). The event catalog includes PAYMENT.PAYOUTSBATCH.SUCCESS/PROCESSING/DENIED and PAYMENT.PAYOUTS-ITEM.BLOCKED/HELD/RETURNED/REFUNDED, none of which HANDOFF subscribes to. Payouts spells `.CANCELED` with one L, while Invoicing spells `INVOICING.INVOICE.CANCELLED` with two ([event names](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/event-names/), Medium). Finally, no evidence was found that Payouts can send PYUSD; the schema takes only ISO-4217 currencies (Medium).

## The whitespace narrows to a PayPal-native, verifiable labor mandate

PayPal's own Resources page names the concept as inspiration. It suggests "a scheduling assistant that pays contractors automatically when a job is marked complete", and separately a payout tool for gig workers that pays the moment a task is approved ([Resources](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/resources), High). The gallery is unpublished ([gallery](https://paypalaihackathon.devpost.com/project-gallery), High), and 13,720 people have registered (High). A basic "AI approves the task, then PayPal pays" entry will therefore probably be common. Differentiation has to come from the mandate, the policy engine, the receipts and the resistance to injection (inference).

Outside the hackathon the field is crowded but split. There are at least twelve **agent-to-human labor marketplaces**. RentAHuman is real and YC Spring 2026 ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/rentahuman), High), and it is crypto-first ([AOL/Gizmodo](https://www.aol.com/articles/rent-human-wants-ai-agents-234839873.html), Medium). HireForHumans advertises no KYC ([HireForHumans](https://hireforhumans.com/), High). Others do verify identity: HumanOps runs KYC through Sumsub ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/humanops), High), Terac vets experts by government ID ([Terac](https://www.terac.com/mcp), High), and AgentHands says it pays through Stripe Connect with Stripe-issued 1099-Ks ([dev.to](https://dev.to/agenthandsai/taxes-in-the-agent-economy-the-1099-k-future-nobodys-ready-for-l7a), High that it says so). The old line "they pay in stablecoins with little identity protection" is therefore mostly true but too absolute.

**Agent-payment infrastructure** mostly flows the other way, from agents to merchants and APIs. The one exception is the most serious threat to the old claim. **Payouts.com** markets an MCP server with a `payouts.create` tool, payee KYC/KYB, W-9/W-8/1099/TIN checks, sanctions and AML screening, and a creator-economy use case ([Payouts.com MCP](https://payouts.com/mcp), High). It also markets a preview, invite-only AgentWallet with AP2-mandate limits, human approval gates and an immutable audit ledger, but names no customers ([Payouts.com AI agents](https://payouts.com/ai-agents), High). Its comparison page claims PayPal Payouts has nothing built for AI agents ([Payouts.com vs PayPal](https://payouts.com/compare-paypal-payouts), High that it says so); that is competitor marketing, but it independently confirms the PayPal gap.

Four other players matter. **Upwork's MCP server** (Aug 10, 2026) lets an agent turn a request into a job post, a shortlist and a draft offer, with a human confirming each change ([Snipework summary](https://snipework.com/news/upwork-mcp-server-ai-integration), Medium). **Grade (YC W26)** sells performance-based payroll that pays creators and contractors, markets paying AI agents and contractors based on results, and reports $380k+ paid to creators in 30 days ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/grade), High); PayPal is reportedly among its rails ([YesPress](https://yespress.io/grade-yc-w26), Low/Medium). **Stripe's Sessions 2026** shipped Agent guardrails in preview but no agent-initiated payouts ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/everything-we-announced-at-sessions-2026), High). And **Payman**, once the best-known company selling AI that pays humans, has pivoted to banks ([paymanai.com](https://www.paymanai.com/), High). Payman is prior art, and a sign that the horizontal version was hard to sell.

**The verdict is that the whitespace partially holds (medium-high confidence).** Three claims should be dropped, because a knowledgeable judge could rebut each with a link: that nobody offers compliant fiat agent-to-human payroll, that agent marketplaces have little identity protection, and that spend mandates for agents are new (AP2 mandates, Catena's approved recipients, Stripe's Agent guardrails and Payman's policy engine all exist). **The 1099 tracker is table stakes, not a differentiator.** BILL's W-9 agent has collected 40,000 W-9s for about 10,000 customers ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251028682021/en), Medium), and Ramp files 1099s inside Bill Pay, so the tracker should be presented as a guardrail, not a feature claim.

What remains unclaimed is an intersection of four things. The first is a **payout-specific mandate**, in which amounts come only from a signed rate card, payees are allowlisted, and weekly and year-to-date counters are enforced in code. The second is an **independent verifier agent** that must pass the work before code may pay. The third is **receipts that a creator, payee, brand or PayPal dispute agent can verify off-platform**. The fourth is that all of it runs on PayPal and Venmo rails, for paying **your own team**, not strangers. A one-line positioning that survives scrutiny: *labor marketplaces settle in stablecoins, and payout incumbents are bolting agent wallets onto closed ledgers; this is the missing Payouts half of PayPal's agent toolkit, where agents can only propose, code pays inside a creator-signed mandate after an independent check, and every dollar carries a receipt anyone can verify.*

## Every 2026 trend converges on "agents propose, code disposes, receipts prove"

### Databricks' "agentic system of record" fits on one Postgres

At Data + AI Summit 2026 (June 15–18), Databricks repositioned the lakehouse as an **"agentic system of record"**. In this model one governed, open-format environment holds operational Postgres data, agent memory, tool and MCP access, model traffic, traces, evals and cost controls under one permission model, and the keynote themes were context, control, cost and choice ([Flexera DAIS recap](https://www.flexera.com/blog/perspectives/databricks-data-ai-summit-2026-recap-genie-one-ltap-lakehouse-rt-and-every-major-launche/), Medium). **Lakebase**, managed Postgres built on the Neon acquisition announced in May 2025 ([Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/databricks-acquires-neon-sees-postgres-scaling-key-agentic-ai), Medium), reached GA on AWS in early February 2026 and on Azure on March 3. It offers roughly 500 ms copy-on-write branches for agent experimentation (Medium). **Unity AI Gateway** is GA as the single entry point for all agent and model traffic, covering MCP server registration, per-group budgets, routing, auditing and MLflow tracing; Ghodsi pitched it as the governance layer for all AI spend and agent activity (Medium). Databricks also says more than 80% of Neon databases are created by agents, a company-supplied figure (Medium). No Databricks source uses the phrase "lakehouse for agents"; the verified framing is "agentic system of record".

The lesson is the principle, not the products, and its five properties all fit on the project's one Render Postgres (inference). First, the signed, hash-chained receipts ledger becomes the **system of record**: task status, payout state and weekly and year-to-date totals become projections, and a test rebuilds them from receipts and compares. Second, every agent becomes a **registered principal**, in an `agents` table holding role, key_id, allowed tools, model_id, prompt_version and revoked_at; this is the analogue of Unity Catalog permissions. Third, **one gateway** handles every agent tool call: `gateway.invoke(agentId, tool, args)` is default-deny, validates arguments with zod and logs a span, mirroring Unity AI Gateway, while Payouts stay reachable only from `packages/policy`. Fourth, **traces and evals sit next to the data** they describe, keyed by prompt, model and policy version. Fifth, **open formats** (RFC 8785 JSON, standard hashes) keep the stack portable; because Lakebase *is* Postgres, a later move to Lakebase branching or Iceberg analytics is a migration, not a rewrite. The pitch line writes itself: *we implemented the agentic-system-of-record pattern on plain Postgres, with seams to Lakebase, AgentCore, Entra Agent ID, AP2 and MCP.* Migrating to Databricks, Snowflake or Neon during the hackathon would spend the 72-hour budget on infrastructure judges never see (inference).

Every other platform is converging on the same stack. AWS AgentCore Policy reached GA on March 3, 2026 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/de/about-aws/whats-new/2026/03/policy-amazon-bedrock-agentcore-generally-available/), High); it intercepts every tool call at a gateway and evaluates Cedar policies before the tool runs ([AWS preview](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/12/amazon-bedrock-agentcore-policy-evaluations-preview), High). Microsoft Entra Agent ID is GA ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/agent-id/whats-new-agent-id), High), and Auth0 for AI Agents shipped Token Vault and async authorization ([Auth0](https://auth0.com/blog/announcing-auth0-for-ai-agents-powering-the-future-of-ai-securely/), High). Google's Memory Bank docs warn explicitly about memory poisoning ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/agent-builder/agent-engine/memory-bank/overview), High), which argues for keeping LLM memory out of anything that sets amounts or payees. OpenAI is winding down Agent Builder and hosted Evals, and neither will be available after Nov 30, 2026 ([mcp.directory](https://mcp.directory/blog/openai-agentkit-deprecation-2026), Medium). That is the strongest argument for keeping agent definitions and evals as versioned code in the repo.

### Injection research already endorses the plan's core invariant

The 2026 consensus is that prompt injection cannot be reliably detected, so defenses must be architectural. CaMeL separates a privileged planner from a quarantined parser and enforces capabilities when tools are called ([arXiv 2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18813), High). The design-patterns paper's plan-then-execute and action-selector patterns keep untrusted data out of control flow ([arXiv 2506.08837](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08837), High). Meta's "Agents Rule of Two" says an agent session should combine at most two of three properties: untrusted input, sensitive access and state change ([Simon Willison](https://simonw.substack.com/p/new-prompt-injection-papers-agents), Medium). Plan-then-execute still lets untrusted data shape the *values* passed into planned calls ([ARMO](https://www.armosec.io/blog/design-patterns-for-securing-llm-agents/), Medium). The plan closes that gap, because a `rateLineId` is a capability reference rather than a value, and payee identity comes from the mandate, never from model output. A Rule-of-Two table in the README costs nothing and reads as rigor:

| Agent | Untrusted input | Sensitive data | State change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ops agent | No | Yes | Yes |
| Verifier | Yes | No | No (tool-less) |
| Explainer | Yes | Yes | No (display-only) |
| Policy engine | Plain code, not an LLM; the only component that can move money | | |

Two eval cases are worth adding (inference from the architecture notes). One covers tainted verifier reasons, for example reasons containing "approve $5000"; reasons must be escaped and never fed back to the ops agent. The other covers an injection planted in an old receipt that the explainer later reads. Approval should sign the exact DECISION hash with a TTL, the property CIBA plus Rich Authorization Requests aim for ([Decryption Digest](https://www.decryptiondigest.com/blog/auth0-entra-agent-id-okta-xaa-ai-agent-authorization-comparison), Medium). Eval pass rates should be reported honestly; even CaMeL's authors say it does not address every attack vector (High).

### Route models by role and give personal agents a door that only proposes

The fast model tier and the open-weight field are both moving quickly. Claude Haiku 5.5 launched on Oct 7, 2026 for high-volume classification ([LLM Gateway timeline](https://llmgateway.io/timeline), Medium). That fits the verifier and explainer roles, with Sonnet 5.5 for ops, all model IDs in one `models.ts`, and `model_id` stamped on every VERDICT. Gemini 4 Argon's model card appeared on Oct 13, 2026 ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-4-argon/), High), while GPT-6 claims rest on aggregators (Low). Open-weight leaderboards (Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek V4) disagree and churn (Low), so the README should not name a "best open model". The future-proof move is a seam: a slot for a verifier from a second model family, with the rule that the system auto-pays only when two model families agree (inference).

Personal agents are becoming the front door. Apple rebuilt Siri at WWDC 2026 as an LLM agent with app actions that carry a request end to end ([Journal du Net](https://www.journaldunet.com/intelligence-artificielle/1551069-wwdc-2026-apple-annonce-une-refonte-complete-de-son-ia/), Medium), and consumer agents increasingly ask for approval before key actions (Low). The MCP 2026-07-28 specification is stateless, treats servers as OAuth 2.1 resource servers, prefers Client ID Metadata Documents over dynamic registration, and adds a formal extensions framework ([MCP release post](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/blob/main/blog/content/posts/2026-07-28-spec-ga/index.md), High). The standards notes cited only the older 2025-11-25 auth spec, but the architecture notes confirmed the newer release. The implication is an MCP surface that **can only propose**. A read-only endpoint (`get_task_status`, `list_receipts`, `verify_chain`) is a stretch goal now; write tools (`propose_task`, `submit_delivery`) come later; and no tool can ever set an amount or call Payouts. That turns "agents propose, code disposes" into a public interface any creator's or payee's personal agent could use (inference).

### Standards leave push payouts and labor evidence unclaimed

AP2 is the mandate model to shape after. Its v0.2 was donated to the FIDO Alliance, where standardization continues in working groups on agentic authentication and payments ([ap2-protocol.org](https://ap2-protocol.org/), High; [FIDO](https://fidoalliance.org/pymnts-google-and-mastercard-contribute-agentic-commerce-standards-to-fido-alliance/), Medium). AP2 defines open mandates (the user's constraints for autonomous execution) and closed mandates (one finalized amount) for checkout and payment (High). It supports only pull payments today, with **push payments on the roadmap**; its payee is a Merchant object, and verifiers must reject unknown constraints ([AP2 Payment Mandate](https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/payment_mandate/); [AP2 Agent Authorization](https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/agent_authorization/), High). Google's UCP, which PayPal endorses, carries AP2 mandates in its 2026-08-25 version and signs them as detached JWS with **ES256, ES384 or ES512** over RFC 8785 canonical JSON ([UCP AP2 extension](https://ucp.dev/specification/payment/extensions/ap2-mandates/), High). An Ed25519-signed mandate is therefore AP2-*shaped*, not conformant. ACP's delegated-payment allowance allows only `one_time`, bound to a single checkout ([ACP spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/agentic-commerce-protocol/agentic-commerce-protocol/main/spec/2026-04-17/openapi/openapi.delegate_payment.yaml), High), so it offers no standing authority. Mastercard's Verifiable Intent draft lists "payee" as a constraint separate from "merchant" but leaves it undefined ([verifiableintent.dev](https://verifiableintent.dev/), High). **No standard defines a mandate for an agent paying a person for work.**

Evidence formats are equally open. A-Comm's Evidence Protocol (AEP), at draft v1.0.3-rc.2, chains JCS-canonicalized artifacts by SHA-256 and seals them with Ed25519, which is nearly the plan's receipt construction, but it **covers neither payouts nor labor** ([AEP GitHub](https://github.com/A-Comm-Tech/a-comm-evidence-protocol), High). SCITT is now RFC 9943, with COSE Receipts in RFC 9942 ([RFC 9943](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9943.html), High). An individual IETF draft for AI-agent action receipts uses the same JCS + SHA-256 + prevHash + Ed25519 construction, and it concedes that **deleting records from the end of the chain is undetectable without checkpoints** ([draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt/), High). On the legal side, Goodwin argues that platforms need a retrievable record of what the principal reviewed and authorized, that a checkbox does not suffice, and reports that Visa requires agentic providers to keep a defensible chain of authorization ([Goodwin, Jun 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/insights-technology-aiml-authorizing-agentic-payments), High for Goodwin's characterization).

The labor flow inverts commerce's order of events. Delivery and verification happen **before** the payment is sealed, so this project's evidence is stronger than any purchase receipt (inference). The concrete design follows (inference). The MANDATE becomes an *AP2 open Payment Mandate profile* (`vct: mandate.payment.open.1`, `profile: x-handoff.labor-mandate.v0`). Standard constraints carry payees, amount range, lifetime budget and dates; namespaced `x-handoff.labor.*` constraints carry the rate card, a weekly budget (AP2's budget is lifetime-only), verification, the approval threshold, screening and tax tracking. Amounts are stored in **integer minor units** and converted to PayPal's decimal strings only at the PayPal boundary. A `cnf` key binding names the policy-engine key, which makes "only the policy engine closes the mandate" a verifiable property, and `presented_terms_hash` and `auth_method` record what the creator saw and how they signed. DECISION plus APPROVAL becomes the closed mandate, and CONFIRMATION becomes a superset of AP2's Payment Receipt carrying PayPal batch and item IDs and the webhook event ID. New REVOCATION and RETURN/UNCLAIMED/REVERSAL records, plus a payee acceptance signature on TASK and DELIVERY, give two-sided evidence for disputes. The pitch claim this earns: *an AP2-shaped labor mandate for push payouts to individuals, the gap AP2 itself lists as roadmap.*

### Post-quantum: the threat to receipts is forgery later, not decryption later

NIST's transition plan (IR 8547, still an initial public draft) **disallows EdDSA, Ed25519 included, after 2035**, and it accepts "dual signatures" as long as one component is NIST-approved ([NIST IR 8547](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2024/NIST.IR.8547.ipd.pdf), High). **Executive Order 14412** (June 22, 2026) requires federal high-value systems to use post-quantum signatures by **Dec 31, 2031**, and orders CISA guidance on a cryptographic bill of materials ([EO 14412](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/eo-14412.pdf), High). Google set 2029 for its own migration ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/03/28/watch-out-bitcoin-devs-google-says-post-quantum-migration-needs-to-happen-by-2029), Medium). The threat itself is not here yet: the largest number honestly factored by a quantum computer is still 15 ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/pq-2025), High). Resource estimates keep falling, though: RSA-2048 needs fewer than one million noisy qubits ([Gidney](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15917v1), High), and 256-bit elliptic curves fewer than 500,000 physical qubits ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/03/31/q-day-just-got-closer-three-papers-in-three-months-are-rewriting-the-quantum-threat-timeline/), Medium). Expert timelines range from the late 2030s to EMVCo's view that a practical threat may not arrive before 2040, if ever ([EMVCo](https://www.emvco.com/knowledge-hub/enabling-seamless-and-secure-payments-in-2026/), Medium).

For receipts that must stay credible through tax and dispute windows, the relevant threat is **forge-later** (inference). A future attacker who derives an actor's Ed25519 key could re-sign a rewritten, self-consistent chain; encryption at rest is not the weak point, because the PII uses AES-256-GCM. Two defenses survive that attacker. One is external anchoring done now: Merkle checkpoints using RFC 9162 hashing ([RFC 9162](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html), High), in the C2SP checkpoint format that already recommends ML-DSA-44 cosignatures ([c2sp.org](https://c2sp.org/tlog-checkpoint), High), published outside the database. The other is a post-quantum co-signature.

The runtime constrains how to add that co-signature. Node 22, the plan's runtime, has no ML-DSA ([Node 22 docs](https://nodejs.org/docs/latest-v22.x/api/crypto.html), High); native support arrived in Node 24.6.0 ([Node docs](https://nodejs.org/api/crypto.html), High). `@noble/post-quantum` works on Node 22, but it is self-audited and makes no constant-time claim ([GitHub](https://github.com/paulmillr/noble-post-quantum), High). That is acceptable for server-held demo keys if the README says so. An ML-DSA-65 signature is 3,309 bytes (High), so the checkpoints should be co-signed, not every receipt. RFC 9964 standardizes the JOSE name "ML-DSA-65" and COSE algorithm ID -49 ([datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-cose-dilithium/), High). Midnight's proofs are PLONK/KZG over BLS12-381 and **not post-quantum** ([docs.rs midnight-proofs](https://docs.rs/midnight-proofs), Medium), so the quantum story must rest on hashes and ML-DSA, not Midnight. A defensible stage line: *no quantum computer can break today's signatures, but NIST disallows Ed25519 after 2035 and federal signatures must be post-quantum by 2031, so every checkpoint is already co-signed with ML-DSA-65 under NIST's dual-signature guidance.*

### Money and regulation: rails stay PayPal, deadlines cluster in 2027

Tax rules shape the tracker. The 1099-NEC threshold rose to **$2,000 for payments after Dec 31, 2025** and is indexed after 2026 ([IRS IRB 2026-19](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-19_IRB), High). Form 1099-K is required only above **$20,000 and 200 transactions** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000), High). Payments through third-party networks are **excluded from 1099-NEC** and reported on 1099-K instead ([IRS i1099mec](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec), High). PayPal applies lower state thresholds: $600 in Vermont, Massachusetts, Virginia and Maryland, and more than $1,000 with 4+ transactions in Illinois ([PayPal Help](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/current-form-1099-k-reporting-thresholds-2025-update-help1131), High). Backup withholding of 24% applies immediately when a contractor has given no TIN ([IRS W-9 requester instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw9), High). The app holds no funds and so cannot withhold; its correct response is to **block the payout and explain why**. A worked example makes a strong demo beat (inference). An editor paid $500 per video weekly earns $26,000 a year across 52 transactions, too few for a 1099-K and possibly outside 1099-NEC too, yet the income is taxable. The tracker should therefore show **two meters**, a $2,000 NEC meter and a 1099-K meter with state thresholds, labeled as running totals and not a filing determination.

Sanctions and money transmission set hard lines. OFAC publishes the SDN list free in CSV and XML with no registration ([OFAC SLS](https://sanctionslist.ofac.treas.gov/Home/static/sdn.html), High). Screening operates under strict liability with risk-based guidance (Medium), so the screen should stamp the list file's hash on each clearance, route near-matches to a human, and be labeled as a demo. FinCEN's payment-processor exemption has four elements ([FIN-2013-R002](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_ruling/FIN-2013-R002.pdf), High). California's DFPI considers the federal conditions irrelevant to its own test ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/rules-enforcement/laws-and-regulations/opinion-letters-by-law-subject/opinion-letter-payment-processing-service-not-exempt-as-agent-of-payee-or-factoring), High), and thirty-one states have adopted the MTMA in whole or part ([CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/node/231461), High). The app must never hold, pool or route funds, and brand invoice money must land in the creator's own PayPal account.

AI-specific rules converge on 2027. The CFPB withdrew its proposed Reg E interpretive rule on emerging payment mechanisms in May 2025 and has said nothing specific about agents since ([NASCUS](https://www.nascus.org/summary-cfpb-withdrawal-of-proposed-interpretive-rule-electronic-funds-transfers-emerging-payment-mechanisms/), Medium). The FTC chair says people who instruct agents are responsible for them and that false safety claims fall under deception law ([Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/ftc-chairman-ferguson-rejects-idea-of-ai-agents-acting-on-their-own/), Low–Medium), which is a reason to stop calling the product "compliant". California's ADMT rules require compliance from **Jan 1, 2027**, and their "significant decisions" explicitly include independent-contracting opportunities and compensation ([Thompson Coburn](https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/californias-admt-rules-under-the-ccpa-what-they-mean-for-significant-decisions-and-how-to-get-ahead-of-implementation/), Medium), which is exactly what the verifier's no-pay verdicts touch. Colorado's narrower SB 26-189 also takes effect Jan 1, 2027 ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/colorado-scales-back-ai-law-with-targeted-implications-for-health-care), Medium). In the EU, the AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties have applied since **Aug 2, 2026** ([AI Act Service Desk](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/faq/when-does-ai-act-go-effect-what-its-timeline-implementation), High). The Digital Omnibus moved Annex III high-risk duties, which cover AI that allocates tasks or evaluates workers' performance, to **Dec 2, 2027** ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force), Medium). The EU Platform Work Directive must be transposed by Dec 2, 2026 ([European Parliament](https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1794723), Medium).

The cheap responses are design choices (inference): an AI disclosure on every outbound agent message, human review of verifier rejections with a contractor appeal path, contractors who accept or decline tasks, and deliverable-based specs that never monitor how or when work is done. That last point matters because both the IRS and the DOL tests center on control ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee), High; [Holland & Knight on DOL](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/dol-proposes-to-rescind-biden-era-independent-contractor-rule), Medium).

Stablecoins argue for a seam, not a feature. The GENIUS Act was enacted July 18, 2025 ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html), High), and because regulators missed the rulemaking deadline it takes effect in practice on **Jan 18, 2027** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/408843/us-regulators-miss-genius-act-deadline), Medium). Mastercard added PYUSD to on-chain settlement on June 3, 2026 ([Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/june/mastercard-expands-settlement-capabilities-to-include-stablecoin.html), High), and Deel and Stripe launched a stablecoin wallet for contractors in June 2026 ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/deel-and-stripe), High). But PYUSD supply fell from about $4.2B in March 2026 to roughly $2.7–3.0B ([Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/paypals-pyusd-q2-2026-report-supply-adoption-and-key-metrics/), Low), and Payouts shows no PYUSD support (Medium). The plan should add a `rail` field (PAYPAL, VENMO, and later PYUSD) and describe PYUSD settlement as roadmap and unverified. Cross-border contractors are the likeliest first users of stablecoin payouts by 2030; US-domestic contractors are less likely to switch, since they already get fast dollars (inference).

### The creator beachhead is real but should be cited as a projection

Goldman Sachs' April 2023 projection is the anchor. It put the creator economy at **$250B, reaching about $480B by 2027**, with about 50 million creators, of whom about **4%** earn more than $100,000, and with brand deals making up about 70% of creator revenue ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027), High, a 2023 projection). ## Pitch the missing half, not the idea PayPal already seeded

"Build for the future, ship the solution now" wins only if every future-facing claim is backed by something running. Judges should see the dual signature verify, open the AP2-profile mandate file, and watch `pnpm verify` catch a truncated chain against an externally anchored checkpoint. The framing that ties it together is that **the product works with human approval today and turns autonomy up as trust is earned, without a rebuild** (inference).

The opening should be **"PayPal's agents can buy; they can't yet pay people"**, followed by the Visa 60% statistic. The pitch should then name PayPal's own seeded idea and show the four things beyond it: the creator-signed labor mandate, the independent verifier gate, deterministic code as the only path to Payouts, and receipts anyone can verify. That answers the Innovation criterion's question about difference from existing concepts head-on, instead of letting a judge discover the overlap. For Potential Impact, the pitch should stay concrete: creator studios paying recurring editors and designers, PayPal and Venmo payees with no crypto involved, and the editor whose $26,000 a year triggers no 1099-K. The primary target is a Grand Prize. The fallbacks are **Best Use of Agentic Commerce** (framing agent-initiated, policy-bound disbursement as the missing half of agentic commerce), Most Impactful, or Best Use of PayPal + AI, with AG Grid as the sponsor prize (inference). ## Conclusion

The research flips the project's center of gravity. The agent is the commodity: Upwork, Payouts.com and a dozen marketplaces already let agents hire people. The **evidence** is the scarce asset. Three independent gaps meet at one point: PayPal's toolkit has no Payouts tool, AP2 names push payments as unfinished, and every evidence format is a draft that ignores labor. A verifier-gated payout whose receipts anyone can check offline sits exactly at that point. That makes Merkle checkpoints, ML-DSA co-signatures and an AP2-shaped labor mandate more than polish: they are the parts of the build no competitor shows and no judge can find elsewhere, and they cost less than the Midnight work they replace.

The second insight is about timing. Consumer trust in agent payments is flat or falling while agent traffic rises 1,700%. Meanwhile California's ADMT rules, Colorado's AI law and the GENIUS Act all take effect in January 2027, and the EU's high-risk duties follow in December 2027. A product built around human approval and contestable verdicts is aligned with where the rules are heading, not working around them. The pitch should say so plainly: autonomy will be earned, and this layer is how it gets earned, one signed receipt at a time.
